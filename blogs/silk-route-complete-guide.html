Silk Route Tourism Guide 2026: Complete Travel Guide to Zuluk, Nathang Valley, Sillerygaon & Aritar

Hidden in the eastern Himalayas of Sikkim lies one of India's most spectacular mountain journeys—the Silk Route. Unlike crowded destinations such as Darjeeling, Shimla, or Manali, the Silk Route offers raw Himalayan beauty, winding mountain roads, remote villages, snow-covered landscapes, ancient trade history, and breathtaking sunrise views over Kanchenjunga.

The Silk Route was once part of the legendary ancient trade network that connected India with Tibet and China. Traders carrying silk, wool, salt, horses, and precious goods crossed these rugged mountain passes for centuries. Today, this historic route has transformed into one of the most rewarding offbeat travel destinations in Northeast India.

The modern Silk Route circuit stretches through picturesque villages such as Sillerygaon, Aritar, Rongli, Zuluk, Lungthung, Tukla, Nathang Valley, Kupup, and Tsomgo Lake. Every turn reveals dramatic Himalayan scenery, changing landscapes, prayer flags fluttering in the wind, and views that leave visitors speechless.

Whether you are a photographer, nature lover, adventure seeker, honeymoon couple, biker, or family traveler, the Silk Route offers an unforgettable Himalayan experience.

Why Visit the Silk Route?

Many travelers ask why they should choose the Silk Route instead of popular destinations such as Darjeeling, Gangtok, Shimla, or Manali. The answer lies in its untouched beauty, historic significance, and unique Himalayan landscapes.

Spectacular Mountain Roads

The famous Zig Zag Road near Zuluk consists of more than 30 hairpin bends and is widely regarded as one of the most scenic mountain roads in India.

Snow-Covered Landscapes

During winter, destinations such as Nathang Valley, Lungthung, Kupup, and Tukla transform into a snowy paradise attracting travelers from across the country.

Unmatched Kanchenjunga Views

Sunrise over Kanchenjunga from Lungthung and Thambi View Point is considered one of the most breathtaking Himalayan experiences in Eastern India.

Offbeat Himalayan Villages

Villages like Sillerygaon, Aritar, and Zuluk provide an authentic mountain experience far away from crowded commercial hill stations.

Rich Historical Importance

The Silk Route once served as a vital trade corridor between India and Tibet, making it one of the most historically significant routes in the Himalayas.

Understanding the Silk Route Circuit

Many travelers assume Silk Route is a single destination. In reality, it is a complete circuit consisting of several villages, mountain passes, viewpoints, and historical locations spread across East Sikkim.

The route gradually ascends from the lower Himalayan valleys to the high-altitude landscapes near the Indo-China border.

Most travelers complete the entire Silk Route circuit in 4 to 6 days depending on their itinerary and sightseeing preferences.

Sillerygaon: The New Darjeeling of East Himalayas

Perched at approximately 6,000 feet above sea level, Sillerygaon is often referred to as the "New Darjeeling." This peaceful mountain village offers stunning Himalayan views, traditional village life, and a refreshing escape from urban crowds.

Surrounded by pine forests and terraced hillsides, Sillerygaon remains one of the most beautiful offbeat destinations near the Silk Route.

Why Sillerygaon is Special

The village enjoys uninterrupted views of the Kanchenjunga range. On clear mornings, the first rays of sunlight illuminate the snow-capped peaks, creating a magical golden panorama.

Ramitey View Point

Ramitey View Point offers one of the finest views of the Teesta River as it winds through the valleys below. It is considered one of the most scenic viewpoints in the Eastern Himalayas.

Damsang Fort

Built by the Lepcha rulers, Damsang Fort is a historically significant site surrounded by beautiful forests and mountain scenery.

Village Walks

Exploring the village on foot allows visitors to experience local culture, traditional homes, organic farming practices, and peaceful Himalayan landscapes.

Best Time to Visit Sillerygaon

  • October to December – Crystal-clear mountain views
  • January to February – Chilly winter weather
  • March to April – Blooming rhododendrons
  • June to September – Lush green monsoon landscapes

Aritar: Gateway to the Silk Route

Aritar serves as the starting point for most Silk Route journeys. Located at an altitude of approximately 4,600 feet above sea level, this peaceful Himalayan town is known for its beautiful lake, ancient monasteries, lush forests, and panoramic mountain views.

Surrounded by rolling hills and traditional Sikkimese villages, Aritar offers travelers a gentle introduction to the breathtaking landscapes that await further along the Silk Route circuit.

Aritar Lake (Lampokhari Lake)

The biggest attraction of Aritar is the picturesque Lampokhari Lake, commonly known as Aritar Lake. This emerald-green water body is believed to be one of the oldest natural lakes in Sikkim and remains a favorite destination among tourists and photographers.

Things to Do at Aritar Lake

  • Boating on the peaceful lake
  • Photography and nature walks
  • Birdwatching
  • Relaxing beside the lakeside promenade
  • Enjoying mountain reflections on clear days

Mankhim View Point

Located above Aritar Lake, Mankhim View Point offers spectacular panoramic views of the Eastern Himalayas and the surrounding valleys.

On clear mornings, visitors can witness breathtaking sunrise views over the Kanchenjunga range while overlooking the beautiful Lampokhari Lake below.

Views from Mankhim

  • Aritar Lake
  • Kanchenjunga Range
  • Eastern Himalayan Valleys
  • Dense Pine Forests
  • Traditional Sikkimese Villages

Aritar Monastery

The Aritar Monastery provides visitors with an opportunity to experience the region's Buddhist culture and spiritual heritage. Surrounded by serene mountain landscapes, the monastery remains a peaceful place for meditation and reflection.

Many travelers choose to spend a night in Aritar before continuing their journey toward Rongli, Padamchen, and Zuluk.

Zuluk: The Heart of the Silk Route

If the Silk Route has a centerpiece, it is undoubtedly Zuluk. Situated at approximately 10,000 feet above sea level, this small mountain settlement has become one of the most iconic destinations in East Sikkim.

For decades, Zuluk remained inaccessible to ordinary travelers due to its strategic importance near the Indo-China border. Today, it attracts nature lovers, photographers, bikers, and adventure enthusiasts from across India.

Despite growing popularity, Zuluk has retained its rustic charm, offering visitors an authentic Himalayan experience far from commercial tourism.

The Famous Zig Zag Road

The legendary Zig Zag Road is the most recognizable attraction on the Silk Route. The road climbs dramatically through more than 30 hairpin bends, creating one of the most spectacular mountain drives in India.

Viewed from higher elevations such as Thambi View Point and Lungthung, the road resembles a giant serpent winding across the mountainside.

This location is undoubtedly the most photographed spot on the entire Silk Route circuit.

Sunrise Over Kanchenjunga

One of the biggest highlights of staying in Zuluk is witnessing sunrise over Kanchenjunga. Before dawn, travelers head toward Lungthung and Thambi View Point to watch the Himalayan peaks gradually light up in golden hues.

As sunlight touches the snow-covered mountains, shades of orange, pink, and gold create a magical spectacle that remains unforgettable.

Best Sunrise Viewpoints Near Zuluk

  • Thambi View Point
  • Lungthung
  • Tukla Valley
  • Nathang Valley

Staying in Zuluk

Accommodation in Zuluk primarily consists of traditional homestays, family-run guesthouses, and small mountain lodges.

Travelers should not expect luxury resorts here. Instead, the charm lies in experiencing local hospitality, home-cooked meals, and peaceful mountain surroundings.

Why Stay in Zuluk?

  • Close proximity to sunrise viewpoints
  • Authentic Himalayan village experience
  • Easy access to Nathang Valley
  • Excellent photography opportunities
  • Comfortable homestays with local cuisine

Nathang Valley: The Ladakh of East India

At an altitude of nearly 13,500 feet above sea level, Nathang Valley is one of the highest inhabited settlements in India. Its vast open landscapes, barren mountains, alpine grasslands, and dramatic weather conditions have earned it the nickname "The Ladakh of East India."

Located near the Indo-China border, Nathang Valley offers visitors a completely different landscape from the lush forests seen in lower elevations.

The valley remains one of the most breathtaking locations along the entire Silk Route circuit.

Why Nathang Valley is Special

Unlike most Himalayan destinations in Eastern India, Nathang features expansive high-altitude plains and open landscapes stretching as far as the eye can see.

Throughout the day, changing sunlight transforms the valley into a constantly evolving canvas of gold, bronze, red, and purple hues.

Photographers often spend hours capturing the changing moods of the valley.

Winter in Nathang Valley

Between December and February, Nathang Valley transforms into a magical snow-covered wonderland. Snow blankets the grasslands, mountains, roads, and rooftops, creating one of the most spectacular winter destinations in Eastern India.

Winter Highlights

  • Heavy snowfall
  • Frozen landscapes
  • Snow-covered mountain roads
  • Sub-zero temperatures
  • Spectacular winter photography opportunities

Sunrise at Nathang Valley

Many travelers believe the sunrise at Nathang Valley surpasses even the famous viewpoints near Darjeeling and Gangtok. The combination of high altitude, vast open terrain, and dramatic lighting creates an extraordinary visual experience.

As the first rays of sunlight sweep across the valley, the surrounding mountains glow in shades of gold and crimson, creating a scene that feels almost unreal.

Kupup: Home of the Elephant Lake

Located at an altitude of approximately 13,000 feet, Kupup is one of the most scenic destinations along the Silk Route. Surrounded by barren mountains and high-altitude grasslands, this remote settlement offers breathtaking Himalayan landscapes.

Kupup is best known for its famous Elephant Lake, one of the highest lakes in India.

Elephant Lake (Kupup Lake)

When viewed from higher elevations, the shape of the lake resembles an elephant, which is how it earned its nickname. The lake remains frozen during severe winter months and becomes surrounded by snow-covered mountains.

Things to See in Kupup

  • Elephant Lake
  • Old Silk Route landscapes
  • Yak grazing fields
  • Army settlements
  • High-altitude Himalayan scenery

Baba Harbhajan Singh Mandir

One of the most unique attractions in East Sikkim is the Baba Harbhajan Singh Mandir. The shrine is dedicated to Indian Army soldier Harbhajan Singh, who is believed by many soldiers to continue protecting the region even after his death.

The temple attracts tourists, pilgrims, and army personnel throughout the year.

Besides its spiritual significance, the surrounding mountain scenery makes the visit worthwhile.

Tsomgo Lake: Jewel of East Sikkim

Located at an altitude of approximately 12,400 feet, Tsomgo Lake (Changu Lake) is one of Sikkim's most famous tourist attractions.

The glacial lake changes its appearance throughout the seasons, offering a completely different experience every time of the year.

Activities at Tsomgo Lake

  • Yak rides
  • Snow activities during winter
  • Photography
  • Mountain sightseeing
  • Tea stalls and local snacks

Most travelers visit Tsomgo Lake while returning from the Silk Route toward Gangtok.

Silk Route Permit Information

Since much of the Silk Route lies close to the Indo-China border, permits are mandatory for all visitors.

Required Documents

  • Government-issued photo ID
  • Passport-size photographs
  • Vehicle documents
  • Hotel booking details

Permits are generally arranged by local tour operators, travel agencies, or homestay owners.

Silk Route Weather Guide

March to May

Pleasant weather with blooming rhododendrons, clear mountain views, and comfortable sightseeing conditions.

June to September

Monsoon brings lush greenery but also frequent fog, landslides, and road closures.

October to December

Widely considered the best season with crystal-clear Himalayan views and pleasant temperatures.

January to February

Peak snowfall season. Temperatures frequently drop below freezing, especially in Nathang Valley and Kupup.

Snowfall Guide for Silk Route

Best Places for Snowfall

  • Nathang Valley
  • Lungthung
  • Kupup
  • Tukla Valley
  • Thambi View Point
  • Zuluk

January is generally considered the most reliable month for experiencing snowfall along the Silk Route.

However, heavy snowfall can occasionally result in road closures and itinerary changes.

Suggested 5 Days Silk Route Itinerary

  • Day 1: NJP / Bagdogra → Sillerygaon
  • Day 2: Sillerygaon → Aritar → Rongli → Padamchen
  • Day 3: Padamchen → Zuluk → Lungthung → Nathang Valley
  • Day 4: Nathang Valley → Kupup → Baba Mandir → Tsomgo Lake → Gangtok
  • Day 5: Gangtok → NJP / Bagdogra

How to Reach the Silk Route

By Air

Bagdogra Airport is the nearest airport.

By Train

New Jalpaiguri (NJP) Railway Station is the nearest major railhead.

By Road

Shared vehicles and private cabs are available from NJP, Siliguri, and Bagdogra.

Travel Tips for Silk Route

  • Carry warm clothing throughout the year.
  • Keep identity documents handy for permit checks.
  • Carry sufficient cash as ATMs are limited.
  • Stay hydrated to minimize altitude discomfort.
  • Start sightseeing early for clear mountain views.
  • Mobile connectivity may be limited in remote areas.
  • Avoid rushing due to high altitude.

Conclusion

The Silk Route is not merely a destination—it is one of the most scenic journeys in the Eastern Himalayas. From the peaceful charm of Sillerygaon and Aritar to the dramatic hairpin bends of Zuluk and the high-altitude wilderness of Nathang Valley, every section of the route offers unforgettable experiences.

Whether you seek snow-covered landscapes, Himalayan sunrises, historic trade routes, photography opportunities, or peaceful mountain villages, the Silk Route delivers an adventure unlike anywhere else in India.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs) About Silk Route Tourism

1. What is the best time to visit the Silk Route?

October to December is considered the best time to visit the Silk Route due to clear skies and spectacular mountain views. January and February are ideal for snowfall lovers, while March and April offer pleasant weather and blooming rhododendrons.

2. Is a permit required for the Silk Route?

Yes. Since the Silk Route passes through sensitive border areas in East Sikkim, permits are mandatory for all Indian tourists. Most tour operators and homestay owners arrange permits on behalf of visitors.

3. Can snowfall be seen on the Silk Route?

Yes. Snowfall is common in Nathang Valley, Lungthung, Kupup, Tukla Valley, and Zuluk during December, January, and February. January is generally the best month to experience heavy snowfall.

4. How many days are required for a Silk Route trip?

A 4 to 6-day itinerary is ideal for covering Sillerygaon, Aritar, Zuluk, Nathang Valley, Kupup, Baba Mandir, and Tsomgo Lake comfortably.

5. Is the Silk Route suitable for senior citizens?

Yes, but senior citizens should consult a doctor before traveling due to high-altitude locations such as Nathang Valley and Kupup. Proper acclimatization and hydration are recommended.

6. Which is better: Silk Route or Darjeeling?

Darjeeling is ideal for heritage charm and urban conveniences, while the Silk Route is perfect for travelers seeking offbeat destinations, dramatic mountain roads, snowfall, and untouched Himalayan landscapes.

7. What is the altitude of Nathang Valley?

Nathang Valley is situated at approximately 13,500 feet above sea level, making it one of the highest inhabited settlements in India.

8. Is the Silk Route safe for families?

Yes. The Silk Route is considered safe for families, honeymooners, solo travelers, and groups. However, visitors should be prepared for long mountain drives and changing weather conditions.

9. What are the main attractions of the Silk Route?

Major attractions include Sillerygaon, Aritar Lake, Zuluk Zig Zag Road, Thambi View Point, Lungthung, Nathang Valley, Kupup Lake, Baba Mandir, and Tsomgo Lake.

10. Which mobile networks work on the Silk Route?

BSNL generally offers the widest coverage. Airtel and Jio work in some areas but connectivity can be limited in remote villages and high-altitude regions.

11. Can I drive my own car on the Silk Route?

Yes, self-drive trips are possible. However, drivers should be experienced in mountain driving because the route includes steep climbs, sharp hairpin bends, fog, and occasional snowfall.

12. Is Nathang Valley colder than Gangtok?

Yes. Nathang Valley is significantly colder than Gangtok due to its much higher altitude. Winter temperatures often fall below freezing and snowfall is common.
